## 1. Project objective

Design an in-product experience that helps meeting commitments reach honest closure.

The case study specifies:
- Baseline: 28% of commitments resolved within 14 days.
- Target: 45% resolved within 14 days within six months.
- A commitment is resolved when it is explicitly marked Done, Reassigned, or Deliberately dropped.
- Silent inaction does not count as resolution.

These are assignment targets, not proven outcomes of the proposed design.

## 2. Selected product direction

Keep the solution consistent with the previous capstone steps.

### Core solution 1: Owner-confirmed commitment handoff

An extracted action item is a candidate, not automatically an accepted commitment.

The proposed owner should be able to:
- Review the candidate and its meeting context.
- Confirm it as a genuine commitment.
- Edit inaccurate or unclear wording.
- Decline it if no commitment was actually agreed.
- Suggest another owner without assigning work to someone who has not agreed.

A new owner must confirm responsibility before the commitment becomes active under their ownership.

### Core solution 2: Lightweight explicit-resolution workflow

Once a commitment is confirmed, its owner should be able to:
- Mark it Done.
- Request reassignment, with the new owner confirming acceptance.
- Deliberately drop it when it is no longer needed.
- Mark it Blocked when progress is prevented by a dependency.

Blocked is not a resolved state.

The experience should minimize manual work and irrelevant notifications, respect user autonomy, and connect with existing workflows rather than becoming another project-management platform.

## 4. Prototype requirements

Build a functional, clickable, browser-based prototype with realistic example data.

Prioritize the actual user journey over decorative dashboard elements.

The prototype should demonstrate these screens or states, integrated into the reference interface:

### A. Existing Tasks experience
Show where the proposed feature belongs within the actual Fireflies interface.

### B. Candidate commitment review
Display:
- Proposed action wording.
- Source meeting and relevant context.
- Proposed owner, if known.
- A clear candidate or awaiting-confirmation status.
- Confirm, Edit, Decline, and Suggest another owner actions.

### C. Ownership confirmation
Show the transition from candidate to confirmed commitment.

When another owner is suggested, demonstrate that the new owner must accept before the commitment becomes active under their ownership.

### D. Active commitment
Show the confirmed commitment with its owner, status, source meeting, and relevant timing information.

### E. Explicit resolution
Demonstrate:
- Done.
- Reassignment request and pending acceptance.
- Deliberately dropped.
- Blocked, which remains unresolved.

Make the difference between a resolved outcome and an unresolved status visually clear.

### F. Status-aware follow-up
Where supported by the proposed design, demonstrate that reminders stop or adapt after a commitment is resolved, declined, or transferred.

Do not imply that real notifications are sent by a local prototype.

### Interaction quality
- Buttons must perform their intended actions.
- State changes must be reflected in the interface.
- Users must be able to navigate between relevant views.
- Provide clear feedback after important actions.
- Include useful empty, pending, blocked, and resolved states where appropriate.
- Make the prototype usable at desktop and mobile widths.
- Avoid dead buttons, decorative-only controls, and misleading interactions.

Use synthetic example data and label it as such. Do not invent research findings or represent fictional examples as real participants.

## 6. Mini PRD requirements

Create a professional Mini PRD that describes the same experience implemented in the prototype.

Include these sections:

1. **Document overview** — project name, author, objective, and scope.
2. **Problem statement** — consistent with the previous capstone steps.
3. **Evidence and rationale** — distinguish case-study facts, research observations, hypotheses, and recommendations.
4. **Goals and success criteria** — include the 14-day resolution rate and the 28% baseline / 45% target.
5. **Target users and needs** — individual contributors, commitment makers, managers, administrators, and buyers where relevant.
6. **Scope** — in-scope features and explicit exclusions.
7. **Current commitment lifecycle** — show the existing or case-study-described journey and potential loss points.
8. **Proposed user flow** — match the prototype.
9. **User stories** — explain what each user needs and why.
10. **Functional requirements** — specify observable product behavior.
11. **Acceptance criteria** — write testable Given/When/Then criteria for the key interactions.
12. **States and transitions** — candidate, awaiting confirmation, open, blocked, pending reassignment, and resolved outcomes.
13. **Edge cases** — false-positive candidates, declined ownership, invalid wording, reassignment rejection, deleted tasks, stale reminders, and abandoned confirmation.
14. **Metrics and event tracking** — include the North Star outcome, supporting metrics, guardrails, and event definitions.
15. **Risks and mitigations** — include autonomy, trust, notification fatigue, inaccurate tasks, and surveillance concerns.
16. **Dependencies and assumptions** — distinguish verified capabilities from unverified requirements.
17. **Prototype limitations** — identify simulated behavior and unavailable integrations.
18. **Open questions and validation plan** — identify what requires user testing or technical discovery.

Keep the PRD concise enough to be useful but detailed enough for a senior PM to review the product logic.

## 7. Measurement rules

Use the 14-day commitment resolution rate as the North Star outcome.

Count a commitment as resolved only when it is explicitly marked:
- Done.
- Reassigned after the new owner accepts.
- Deliberately dropped.

Do not count:
- Silent inactivity.
- Unconfirmed candidate tasks.
- Pending reassignment.
- Blocked commitments.

Define the eligible cohort and measurement start point clearly. Explain any ambiguity that must be resolved before implementation.

Include guardrails for:
- Candidate-task quality.
- Owner-confirmation friction.
- Notification dismissals and fatigue.
- User trust and autonomy.
- Inappropriate assignments.
- Deliberate-drop rates and potential metric gaming.

Do not claim the proposed design will increase the resolution rate until supported by experimental evidence.
